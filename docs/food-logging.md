# Food logging

The Food tab (`/food`) logs Indian home food the way people eat it: by the katori, roti, glass
and ladle, with light, normal or rich oil, as a whole thali, and the usual meal in one tap.
Logging and daily totals are free; Pro (`food_insights`) adds real maintenance, food trends on
Progress, protein help with Apply, and typing a meal in one line.

## Where the numbers come from

- **Ingredients**: USDA SR28 values per 100 g (public domain), in
  `src/data/library/ingredients.ts`, generated by `scripts/usda-ingredients.mjs` from the SR28
  abbreviated file (`ABBREV.txt`, also in the npm package `fda-nutrient-database@1.0.2`). Each
  carries its NDB number. Proxies are named in a `note` (paneer uses queso blanco, poha uses
  white rice).
- **Dishes**: standard home recipes in grams, in `src/data/library/foods.ts`. Per 100 g cooked
  = sum of ingredient nutrients / cooked weight of the pot. Breads and batters get their cooked
  weight from moisture (34% for breads, as USDA chapati). The cooking fat is kept apart so the
  oil level changes only the fat (0.5, 1, 2 times).
- **Portions**: volume x fill x density (`unitGrams` in `domain/nutrition/calc.ts`). Katori
  150, 200, 250 ml served 80% full; a person can set their own katori size.
- Nothing is copied from INDB or IFCT. They are used to cross-check and are credited.
  Permission emails were drafted for both; the feature does not depend on them.

Every food is tested (`domain/nutrition/library.test.ts`): energy matches protein, carbs and
fat within 12%, realistic portions, oil levels in order, diet labels from ingredients, and
USDA cooked references for rice and chicken. Change a recipe: bump `FOOD_LIBRARY_VERSION`;
logs keep the numbers they were saved with.

## Data

- Dexie v4: `foodLogs` and `customFoods`. Profile gains `dietType` and `katoriMl`.
- Supabase migration `20261021000011_food_logging.sql` (RLS on both tables). **Apply it in

- Writes go through `data/repositories/food.ts` (`logFoods`, `copyFoodLogs`, `updateFoodLog`,

## How it connects

- Home: Food today card with the usual meal for the time of day.
- Body: targets card shows the food log average and real maintenance (Pro) with a target for
  the goal in one tap.
- Progress: Food section (Pro), with an Explain button.
- Workout summary: Refuel card on the day of the workout.
- OverloadGPT: the chat and the targets Explain include food lines (`nutritionLines` in
  `features/coach/brief.ts`).
- Science page: topic `food-values`. Settings: Food CSV export.

## The wider library (October 2026)

- Curated foods in `data/library/foods.ts`: our recipes (`DISHES`, `BASICS`) plus pack labels
  (`LABELS`: whey, plant and yeast protein, soya chunks, peanut butter, oats, muesli, protein
  bars and drinks, everyday snacks), each with `source: 'label'`. Gym staples (egg whites,
  grilled chicken breast, brown rice, quinoa, sweet potato, boiled chana and rajma, soya chunks,
  Greek yogurt, skimmed milk, makhana, sattu, overnight oats, shakes) are in `STARTER_FOODS`
  and the protein suggestions. Dishes were chosen from what India eats and orders most (HCES
  2023-24 spending shares, Swiggy How India Eats 2025).
- The long tail, loaded only on the Food tab and the Add food sheet (`moreFoods.ts`):
  `usdaFoods.json` (about 8,250 USDA SR28 foods with household measures, written by
  `scripts/usda-ingredients.mjs`, baby and native foods left out) and `indbFoods.json` (about
  860 Indian Nutrient Databank dishes, Vijayakumar et al. 2024; dishes that counted all the
  frying oil or whose energy did not add up were dropped). Search ranks curated foods, then
  INDB, then USDA. The meal parser never uses the long tail.

## AI food logging (Pro, `ai_food`)

- Add food opens with "Tell OverloadGPT what you ate". Pro types or speaks (Web Speech, en-IN)
  a meal; `src/app/foodAi.ts` sends `{ kind: 'food', text, diet }` to the `coach` function.
- Server (`_shared/coach.ts`, `handleFoodLog`): Pro checked from the subscription row, the AI
  asked with web search (Gemini Google Search grounding, Groq `browser_search` on GPT-OSS; both
  retry without search on error), the JSON read leniently and every item checked
  (`checkFoodItem`: 1 to 2,000 g, at most 9.5 kcal a gram, macros within the weight, energy
  within 30% of 4/4/9). Nothing is stored.
- The phone (`features/food/AiFoodLog.tsx`) uses a library food when the name matches exactly;
  otherwise the item becomes an `ai` food marked "From the web" or "Estimated". On logging,
  `keepPlateFoods` saves AI foods as the person's own foods so logs can be edited later.
- Offline or AI down: the library parser fills what it can and says so.
- This is the one exception to "No AI for numbers": AI values are always labelled and checked.
- The fake Supabase answers food requests with fixed numbers ("#food-down" makes it fail).

## Photo food logging (Pro, `ai_food`, October 2026)

- Add food: "Let OverloadGPT log it" with Snap a photo and Type or say it. The photo screen
  (`features/food/PhotoFoodLog.tsx`) takes a camera or gallery photo, resizes it on the phone to
  1,280 px JPEG (`lib/photo.ts`, EXIF rotation kept), and sends it once with an optional note
  and up to 160 food names the app knows (`knownNames` in `aiMatch.ts`).
- Server (`_shared/coach.ts`, `handleFoodPhoto`): Pro from the subscription row, then today's
  allowance through `claim_ai_use('photo')` (migration `20261022000012_ai_photo_quota.sql`,
  15 a day per account, India day, given back by `release_ai_use` when every provider fails).
  `FOOD_PHOTO_PROMPT` asks for each food, amounts judged from things of known size (plate,
  katori, roti, glass), visible oil, label reading, and a confidence per item. Gemini gets the
  image inline; Groq uses its vision model (`GROQ_VISION_MODEL`, default `qwen/qwen3.8-27b`).
  Items go through `checkFoodItem` like typed meals. The photo is never stored.
- The phone matches names to the curated library first (library values, AI amount); others are
  "From the photo" or "From the label". Low-confidence items are marked to check. Nothing is
  logged until the person puts it on the plate.
- The fake Supabase answers every photo with 2 rotis, a katori of dal tadka and a low-confidence
  mango pickle; "#photo-down" in the note makes it fail.
